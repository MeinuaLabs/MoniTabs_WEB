How to Ask Someone to Pay You Back (12 Polite Message Templates)

October 2026 · 6 min read

Asking for your own money back shouldn't feel awkward — but it almost always does. The trick is to make the message short, specific and easy to say yes to.

Most people who owe money aren't avoiding you. They forgot, they lost track of the amount, or they're waiting for payday and didn't say so. A clear, friendly message solves the first two and opens the door to the third. Below are 12 templates you can copy, grouped by situation — plus the four rules that make any of them work.

4 rules for asking for money back

  1. Be specific. Name the amount, what it was for and when. "The $60 for concert tickets in March" gets paid; "that money" gets ignored.
  2. Make paying easy. Include how they can pay you — bank transfer, cash, a payment app.
  3. Offer a path, not a demand. If the amount is big, suggest installments. Half now and half next month is still a yes.
  4. Put it in writing. A text or message creates a record of the amount and the date, so nobody has to rely on memory later.

Gentle nudges (first reminder)

1. The casual check-in

Hey! Just a quick reminder about the $60 for the concert tickets. No rush — whenever works for you this week 🙂

2. The "I'm tidying up my budget" message

Hi! I'm going through my budget for the month — could you send the $120 from the trip when you get a chance? My bank details are below.

3. The date reminder

Hey, you mentioned you'd pay back the $200 by the 15th — just checking that still works for you?

Friendly follow-ups (they forgot)

4. The second nudge

Hi again! Bumping this in case my last message got buried — the $60 from March. Thanks!

5. Offering installments

I know $400 is a lot at once. Would $100 a month work for you? Happy to set it up that way.

6. After a partial payment

Got the $100, thank you! That leaves $300. Would the rest by the end of the month work?

Family and close friends

7. For a sibling or parent

Hey, could you send back the $150 for the car repair when you're paid? I'm saving for something this month. Love you!

8. For a close friend

I hate bringing up money, but I need the $250 back for rent this month. Could you send it by Friday?

Coworkers and acquaintances

9. The lunch or gift collection

Hi! Quick one — I covered your share of the team lunch ($18). You can send it whenever, thanks!

10. The professional tone

Hi Sam, a reminder that $75 is still outstanding from the conference booking. Could you settle it by Thursday? Thanks.

When they go quiet

11. Firm but polite

Hi, I've reached out a few times about the $300 from June. I'd really like to settle this — can you pay it by the 30th, or let me know a date that works?

12. The final reminder

I need to close this out: $300 is still owed from June. Please send it by the 30th. If that's not possible, tell me what you can pay and when.

Frequently asked questions

How do you politely ask someone to pay you back?

Keep it short and specific: mention the amount, what it was for and when, add how they can pay you, and offer a date or installments. A friendly, written message works better than bringing it up in person.

How long should I wait before asking for my money back?

If you agreed on a date, remind them on that date. If you didn't, a reminder after two to four weeks is reasonable — the longer you wait, the more awkward it gets and the more likely the details are forgotten.

What if a friend only pays back part of the money?

Thank them, confirm the remaining balance in writing and agree on a date for the rest. Recording each partial payment keeps both of you on the same page.

How can I avoid forgetting who owes me money?

Write it down when you lend it, with a due date and a reminder. A debt tracker app like MoniTabs keeps the balance, payment history and reminders in one place.
